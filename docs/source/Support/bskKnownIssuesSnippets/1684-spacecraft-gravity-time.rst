- GitHub issue 1684: In Basilisk v2.12.0 through v2.12.2, :ref:`spacecraft` clamped negative SOSRI gravity stage times to zero. A moving central body could also shift the reported inertial position when the last integrator stage differed from the output epoch.
  Forwarding an older central-body sample gave it the current task timestamp. In those releases, subscribe directly to the original ephemeris to preserve the sample epoch. Scheduled energy could also describe the integrated state before a prescribed reference was applied.
  An unwritten motion reference could overwrite the initial hub state, and a reference first supplied or changed on the time-zero update could leave stale derivatives. Write the intended initial references before initializing the simulation.
  Connecting an attitude reference to a translation-only spacecraft after initialization could raise an error after integration had already advanced. Leave ``attRefInMsg`` disconnected in this mode.
  With RKF45 or SOSRI, replacing a planet ephemeris on that update could also leave gravity derivatives at their reset values.
  Articulated bodies could publish inertial positions before the integration origin was known. Read their inertial output after the spacecraft has processed the first written central ephemeris and the articulated body has published its updated state.
  A prescribed body could retain the previous hub attitude after a reference changed, and hinge reactions could use the angular rate from before the reference was applied. Scheduling an articulated body faster than its parent dynamics could also give an earlier pose a later timestamp.
  Without motion commands, a prescribed body could lose its configured initial position or rates and extrapolate acceleration from simulation time zero when initialized later.
  Its inertial output and a direct spacecraft energy query could also lag a command read at the spacecraft tick. In the solar-array deployment and screw-motion examples, the effectors read the preceding profiler sample. Schedule the profilers before the effectors at each dynamics tick.
  A constraint between a hub and an articulated body could mix central-body-relative and inertial coordinates, producing spurious loads when the central body had a nonzero inertial position or velocity. Adding a short attachment offset to a much larger parent coordinate could also introduce a spurious constraint load through roundoff.
  A hub and its own branch could also produce spurious loads for coincident connection points. Constraint messages could report the final trial stage under the accepted step's timestamp, with the force sign depending on which parent evaluated last.
  Repeated updates of an active constraint at the same tick could advance the load filters more than once. In the affected releases, schedule the constraint once per dynamics tick after the spacecraft.
  Spacecraft sharing a ``GravBodyData`` could read stale planet properties and report incorrect gravity-gradient torques. In the affected releases, give each spacecraft a separate gravity body subscribed to the same ephemeris.
  Gravity-gradient torque could also use an incorrect separation when a central body had a nonzero inertial position. In those releases, use ephemerides relative to the chosen central body so that its position is zero.
  With ``ExtPulsedTorque`` attached, SOSRI could raise an error on the first step from time zero. For additive-noise models, SOSRA avoids the negative stage.
  In v2.12.0 through v2.12.2, :ref:`MJScene` also converted negative stage times directly to unsigned callback timestamps. For time-dependent SOSRI models, starting far enough after zero to keep all stages nonnegative avoids that conversion. Site timestamps could also lose nanoseconds on long missions.
  A MuJoCo callback enabled by ``extraEoMCall`` could read the previous spacecraft and state-effector telemetry in a synchronized group. In those releases, consumers that need the accepted telemetry can run on a scheduled task after the spacecraft and scene updates.
- In v2.12.0 through v2.12.2, :ref:`thrusterStateEffector` could report the last Runge–Kutta stage's thrust factor and nozzle loads at the accepted tick. A thruster scheduled before its spacecraft could also stamp the previous loads with the upcoming tick. For post-processing in these releases, read the registered state named by ``nameOfKappaState`` after the spacecraft advances and compute the nozzle loads from that factor and the mount geometry.
