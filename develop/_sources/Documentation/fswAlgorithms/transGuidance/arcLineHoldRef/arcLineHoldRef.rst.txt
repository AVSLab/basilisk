Executive Summary
-----------------

``arcLineHoldRef`` publishes a time-varying inertial translation reference for
a specific arc-to-line-to-hold maneuver.  The reference follows a circular
arc in a plane tilted 45 degrees out of the inertial ``x-y`` plane, blends
smoothly onto the inertial ``y`` axis, moves along that axis to a final
position, and then remains stationary.  The module publishes position,
velocity, and acceleration; it does not calculate control forces or move a
spacecraft.

The radius, phase durations, blend endpoint, and final position are
configurable.  The path topology, circle plane, line axis, and terminal hold
are fixed.  This is not a general-purpose trajectory generator.

Module Assumptions and Limitations
----------------------------------

The arc satisfies :math:`r_x=r_z`, fixing its plane at 45 degrees out of the
inertial ``x-y`` plane.  The subsequent straight segment changes only
:math:`r_y`.  Both ``blendEndPosition`` and ``finalPosition`` must therefore
be finite three-component vectors with *exactly zero* inertial ``x`` and
``z`` components.  ``Reset()`` rejects incompatible endpoints with
``BSK_ERROR`` (a ``BasiliskError`` in Python), rather than allowing a position
jump at the blend-to-line or line-to-hold boundary.

``radius``, ``tCircle``, ``tTransition``, and ``tLinear`` must all be finite.
``Reset()`` rejects NaN and positive or negative infinity with ``BSK_ERROR``
(a ``BasiliskError`` in Python) before computing trajectory boundary states.
It also requires ``tTransition > 0``, ``tLinear > 0``, and
``tCircle + tTransition > 0``.  For the intended arc-then-blend sequence,
configure a nonnegative ``tCircle`` and a positive radius.  The latter two
conditions are recommendations, not additional checks performed by the
module.

The phase clock is the absolute simulation time, measured from simulation
time zero.  Calling ``Reset()`` at a later time does not restart the maneuver.
All parameters should be set before ``Reset()``: it precomputes the blend
boundary states, so changing configuration while the module is running can
make adjacent segments inconsistent.

The last moving segment is a quintic curve confined to the ``y`` axis with
zero terminal velocity and acceleration.  For arbitrary choices of the two
``y`` endpoints and phase duration, the intermediate motion is not
guaranteed to be monotonic or to slow down at every instant.


Mathematical Modeling
---------------------

Let :math:`R` be ``radius``, :math:`t_c` be ``tCircle``, :math:`T_b` be
``tTransition``, and :math:`T_l` be ``tLinear``.  The angular rate of the
initial arc is

.. math::

    \omega = \frac{\pi}{t_c + T_b}.

For :math:`0 \leq t < t_c`, the inertial reference position is

.. math::

    \boldsymbol{r}_R(t) = R
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \sin(\omega t)/\sqrt{2} \\
        \cos(\omega t) \\
        \sin(\omega t)/\sqrt{2}
    \end{bmatrix}.

The module evaluates its analytic first and second time derivatives for the
velocity and acceleration references.  In particular,
:math:`\boldsymbol{a}_R=-\omega^2\boldsymbol{r}_R` on the arc.

For :math:`t_c \leq t < t_c+T_b`, a separate quintic polynomial in each
coordinate joins the arc to the line.  At its start, position, velocity,
and acceleration equal the arc states at :math:`t_c`.  At its end, they are

.. math::

    \boldsymbol{r}_b = [0,\,y_b,\,0]^\mathsf{T},\qquad
    \boldsymbol{v}_b = [0,\,\omega R,\,0]^\mathsf{T},\qquad
    \boldsymbol{a}_b = \boldsymbol{0},

where :math:`y_b` is the ``y`` component of ``blendEndPosition``.  Matching
all three states at both ends makes the reference continuous through
acceleration at the arc-to-blend and blend-to-line boundaries.

During the final moving segment, let
:math:`\tau=t-t_c-T_b`, :math:`\Delta y=y_f-y_b`, and
:math:`v_b=\omega R`, where :math:`y_f` is the ``y`` component of
``finalPosition``.  For :math:`0 \leq \tau < T_l`, the reference has
:math:`r_x=r_z=0` and

.. math::

    y(\tau) &= y_b + v_b\tau + a_3\tau^3 + a_4\tau^4 + a_5\tau^5, \\
    a_3 &= \frac{10\Delta y-6v_bT_l}{T_l^3}, \\
    a_4 &= \frac{-15\Delta y+8v_bT_l}{T_l^4}, \\
    a_5 &= \frac{6\Delta y-3v_bT_l}{T_l^5}.

The velocity and acceleration are the analytic derivatives of this
polynomial.  At :math:`t=t_c+T_b+T_l`, the output switches to the final
hold: :math:`\boldsymbol{r}_R=[0,\,y_f,\,0]^\mathsf{T}` and
:math:`\boldsymbol{v}_R=\boldsymbol{a}_R=\boldsymbol{0}`.


User Guide
----------

This section is to outline the steps needed to setup the ``arcLineHoldRef`` module in Python using Basilisk.

#. Import the module::

    from Basilisk.fswAlgorithms import arcLineHoldRef

#. Create an instance::

    module = arcLineHoldRef.ArcLineHoldRef()

#. Set the arc radius and the three phase durations::

    module.setRadius(20.0)  # [m]
    module.setTCircle(5.0 * 3600.0)  # [s]
    module.setTTransition(1.0 * 3600.0)  # [s]
    module.setTLinear(110.0 * 60.0)  # [s]

#. Set the blend endpoint and final hold position.  Their inertial ``x`` and
   ``z`` components must be zero::

    module.setBlendEndPosition([0.0, -16.0, 0.0])  # [m]
    module.setFinalPosition([0.0, -5.0, 0.0])  # [m]

#. Add the module to a task at the rate needed by the reference consumer::

    sim.AddModelToTask(taskName, module)

These example values are also the module defaults, so steps 3 and 4 can be
omitted when those values are desired.  The arc begins at
:math:`[0,R,0]^\mathsf{T}` at simulation time zero.
The blend starts at ``tCircle``, the straight segment starts at
``tCircle + tTransition``, and the stationary hold starts at
``tCircle + tTransition + tLinear``.
