.. _rbkNumericalLimits:

Numerical scope and representation limits
-----------------------------------------

These limits apply to the C rigid-body kinematics routines, the corresponding
Python and Numba functions, and the Rust bindings to C. Angles are in radians;
Euler parameters are scalar-first unit quaternions. Supply valid rotation
matrices and normalized quaternions: the routines do not generally validate or
normalize their inputs.

The library uses ordinary double-precision attitude formulas and supports zero
and small rotations through analytic limits where appropriate. It does not
guarantee accurate finite results for every finite floating-point input.
Very large coordinates or rates can overflow intermediate products, and
extremely small components can underflow. No common exception or non-finite
output convention is guaranteed outside this numerical scope.

- **Modified Rodrigues parameters (MRPs):** normally maintain a norm at or below
  one using ``MRPswitch(sigma, 1.0)``. Modest values above one remain supported,
  including during shadow switching. Conversions retain their documented
  branches: ``MRP2EP`` can return a negative quaternion scalar and ``MRP2PRV``
  can return an angle above pi. ``MRPshadow`` always requests the other set;
  its zero-input shadow is singular, whereas ``MRPswitch`` leaves zero unchanged.
- **Gibbs vectors (classical Rodrigues parameters):** the representation is
  singular at a half-turn. Use quaternions or MRPs when a calculation approaches
  that attitude. This also applies when addition or subtraction produces a
  half-turn, or when converting unit-norm MRPs to Gibbs vectors. Near these
  singularities, cancellation can make a small denominator numerically zero;
  an accurately resolved finite result is not guaranteed.
- **Principal rotation vectors (PRVs):** prefer the principal representation
  with angle at most pi when accumulating rotations. The conversion functions
  retain their existing angle branches and do not automatically reduce every
  result to this interval. The forward PRV rate map is singular at nonzero full
  turns; its zero-angle singularity is removable and is handled analytically.
- **Euler angles:** at a conversion singularity the third angle is set to zero
  and the observable combined rotation is recovered in the first angle.
  This preserves the attitude but does not make the Euler rate map nonsingular.

Angle wrapping supports multiple revolutions, but cannot recover angular detail
already lost to rounding in a very large input. The library does not impose a
new magnitude cutoff or silently switch representations in rate calculations.
Choose a well-conditioned representation before evaluating its derivatives;
an attitude shadow switch also changes the coordinate derivatives.

For Python and Numba input-container and compiled-call requirements, see
:ref:`numbaModules_rbkn`.
