C Module: celestialTwoBodyPoint

Auxiliary Files

  • Unit tests

Executive Summary

This module points one reference-frame axis towards a primary celestial object. The secondary goal is to point a second reference-frame axis as closely as possible towards another celestial object while maintaining the primary alignment.

For example, the reference can guide a sensor towards a planet while keeping a solar panel normal as close as possible to the Sun direction. The module PDF Description contains further information on this module’s function, how to run it, as well as testing.

Module Assumptions and Limitations

The module sets the accelerations of both bodies relative to the spacecraft to zero. Its rate and acceleration outputs therefore describe the instantaneous reference motion under that assumption. Nonzero relative accelerations are not available in the input messages.

If the secondary message is absent, or its direction is within singularityThresh of parallel or antiparallel to the primary direction, the module uses the primary relative position crossed with its relative velocity as the secondary direction. This fallback requires a nonzero cross product; the primary relative position must also be nonzero.

Angular Velocity and Acceleration

The arrays r1_hat_N, r2_hat_N, and r3_hat_N contain the reference basis vectors in inertial N-frame components. Their first and second derivatives are inertial time derivatives, also expressed in N. The rows of dcm_RN are these basis vectors, so this matrix maps N-frame components to R-frame components.

Writing these component arrays as \(\boldsymbol q_i\), the reference-frame components of angular velocity and angular acceleration are

\[\begin{split}{}^R\boldsymbol\omega_{R/N} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol q_3 \cdot \dot{\boldsymbol q}_2 \\ \boldsymbol q_1 \cdot \dot{\boldsymbol q}_3 \\ \boldsymbol q_2 \cdot \dot{\boldsymbol q}_1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad {}^R\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_{R/N} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{\boldsymbol q}_3 \cdot \dot{\boldsymbol q}_2 + \boldsymbol q_3 \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol q}_2 \\ \dot{\boldsymbol q}_1 \cdot \dot{\boldsymbol q}_3 + \boldsymbol q_1 \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol q}_3 \\ \dot{\boldsymbol q}_2 \cdot \dot{\boldsymbol q}_1 + \boldsymbol q_2 \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol q}_1 \end{bmatrix}.\end{split}\]

Differentiating a projection onto a rotating basis also introduces \(\boldsymbol\omega_{R/N}\cdot\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol r}}_i\). This term vanishes because \(\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol r}}_i = \boldsymbol\omega_{R/N}\times\hat{\boldsymbol r}_i\). It must not be evaluated by dotting R-frame rate components with N-frame basis-vector derivatives.

Both outputs are transformed to N-frame components with the transpose:

\[{}^N\boldsymbol\omega_{R/N} = [RN]^T\,{}^R\boldsymbol\omega_{R/N}, \qquad {}^N\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_{R/N} = [RN]^T\,{}^R\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_{R/N}.\]

These are the omega_RN_N and domega_RN_N fields of attRefOutMsg.

Validation

In addition to the single-body and two-body reference checks, the unit tests use central finite differences along straight-line body trajectories. They differentiate the published attitude basis to check the angular rate and the published rate to check angular acceleration. The cases cover a moving secondary body and the absent, parallel, and antiparallel secondary-direction fallbacks at two sample times. A 0.1 s difference step is used with absolute tolerances of \(10^{-11}\) rad/s for rate and \(10^{-13}\) rad/s2 for acceleration.
