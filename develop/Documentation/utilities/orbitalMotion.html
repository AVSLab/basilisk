orbitalMotion.atmosphericDrag(Cd: float, A: float, m: float, rvec: ndarray, vvec: ndarray) → ndarray[source]

Estimate Earth’s drag acceleration opposite the supplied velocity.

The atmosphere is assumed at rest in the input inertial frame; no rotation or wind correction is applied. The atmosphericDensity() fit is valid from 100 to 1000 km. Positive altitudes below this range are extrapolated; above 1000 km its existing exponential tail is retained without a cutoff.

Zero speed, area, coefficient, or computed density gives a zero vector after input validation. Acceleration underflow is allowed. Inputs are not modified; sequences and NumPy arrays with shape (3,) are accepted.

Parameters:

  • Cd – finite, nonnegative drag coefficient (dimensionless)

  • A – finite, nonnegative projected area (m^2)

  • m – finite, positive spacecraft mass (kg)

  • rvec – finite inertial position three-vector above Earth’s equatorial radius (km)

  • vvec – finite inertial velocity three-vector (km/s)

Returns:

drag acceleration in the input frame (km/s^2), as a NumPy three-vector

Raises:

ValueError – for invalid inputs, unrepresentable norms, or acceleration overflow

orbitalMotion.clMeanOscMapChecked(req: float, J2: float, oe: ClassicElements, oep: ClassicElements, sign: int)[source]

Apply the first-order J2 mean/osculating element mapping.

The mapping follows Appendix F of Analytical Mechanics of Space Systems, Schaub and Junkins, fourth edition. Inputs must be finite and elliptic, with inclination in [0, pi], and sufficiently far from the critical inclinations where 1 - 5*cos(i)**2 = 0. The perturbation must be small; applying the opposite mapping leaves errors of second order in J2.

Both equatorial limits are supported. Whole revolutions in true anomaly do not change the mapped Cartesian state; output angles need not retain input revolution counts. With J2 = 0, the six classical elements are copied exactly, including their angle branches. Only a, e, i, Omega, omega, f are written; auxiliary fields are unchanged. Input and output may be the same object.

Parameters:

  • req – equatorial radius, in the same length unit as oe.a

  • J2 – dimensionless second zonal harmonic coefficient

  • oe – input classical elements, with angles in radians

  • oep – output classical elements, with the same units as oe

  • sign – +1 for mean to osculating, -1 for osculating to mean

Returns:

None; the mapped elements are written to oep

Raises:

ValueError – for invalid inputs, singular inclinations or invalid outputs

For nonzero J2, abs(1 - 5*cos(i)**2) <= sqrt(machine epsilon) is rejected. Mapped elements must remain finite and elliptic. This numerical guard does not establish the accuracy of the perturbation model. On failure, oep is unchanged. Zero J2 bypasses the critical-inclination guard only.

orbitalMotion.elem2rv(mu: float, elements: ClassicElements) → Tuple[ndarray, ndarray][source]

Convert classical elements to a finite inertial Cartesian state.

Inclination must be in [0, pi]. Distances use the same unit L as mu. Ellipses have a > 0, e < 1; hyperbolas have a < 0, e > 1. No eccentricity tolerance selects a conic. The C and Python core conversions share these degenerate conventions:

  • Planar parabola: e = 1, a = 0, rPeriap > 0; f is true anomaly.

  • Rectilinear ellipse: e = 1, a > 0; f is eccentric anomaly E, with outward motion for sin(E) > 0.

  • Rectilinear hyperbola: e = 1, a < 0; f is signed hyperbolic anomaly H, with outward motion for H > 0.

  • Rectilinear parabola: e = 1, a = rPeriap = 0; rmag > 0 supplies radius, and f = 0 selects outward motion or f = pi inward motion.

For rectilinear states, i, Omega, omega orient the outward radial ray along the perifocal x axis; the plane about that ray is arbitrary. Collisions and open-conic anomalies at or beyond infinity are rejected. The extra fields are read only where required by these conventions.

Parameters:

  • mu – positive gravitational parameter (L^3/s^2)

  • elements – orbital elements, with distances in L and angles in radians

Returns:

inertial position (L) and velocity (L/s)

Raises:

ValueError – if the elements do not describe a finite physical state

orbitalMotion.jPerturb(rvec: ndarray, num: int, planet: str = 'CELESTIAL_EARTH', *, strict: bool = False) → ndarray | None[source]

Compute a body’s zonal perturbation acceleration through degree num.

Earth includes J2 through J6. Mercury, Venus, Moon, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune include J2 only. Sun and Pluto use a zero-zonal approximation and return a zero vector with strict=True. Phobos, Deimos and unknown body names are unsupported by strict=True. The default retains the original Earth fallback, None for Sun/Pluto, and fractional-degree truncation.

This external gravity expansion is intended for positions outside the body’s reference radius; the surface boundary is not enforced. The input frame must be planet-centered, with its z axis along the spin axis. Central gravity, tesseral terms and other forces are not included. With strict=True, all inputs are validated, including zero-zonal models.

Parameters:

  • rvec – finite, nonzero position vector with shape (3,), in kilometers

  • num – integer maximum included degree, from 2 through 6 (inclusive)

  • planet – body name prefixed by CELESTIAL_; defaults to Earth

  • strict – opt in to checked body/degree selection and zero-vector Sun/Pluto results; the default preserves the original dispatch behavior

Returns:

perturbation acceleration in the input frame, in km/s^2, or the legacy None sentinel for Sun/Pluto when strict=False

Raises:

ValueError – for invalid inputs, unsupported bodies or nonfinite results

orbitalMotion.rv2elem(mu: float, rVec: ndarray, vVec: ndarray, *, canonical: bool = False) → ClassicElements[source]

Convert a Cartesian state to classical orbital elements.

Inputs must be finite and use consistent distance units. Inclination i is in [0, pi]; Omega and omega are in [0, 2*pi). Nonradial true anomaly f is in [0, 2*pi) for ellipses/parabolas and [-pi, pi] for hyperbolas. With canonical=True, degenerate conics use the conventions of elem2rv(). The default retains the supported Python parabolic encoding a = -rPeriap, rApoap = -1.

A dimensionless tolerance of 1e-12 selects singular-angle conventions:

  • For e <= tolerance, omega = 0 and f is the argument of latitude.

  • For sin(i) <= tolerance, Omega = 0 and i is set to 0 or pi. omega measures periapsis from the inertial x axis in the direction of orbital motion. Equatorial retrograde longitudes therefore have the opposite sign to inertial azimuths.

  • If both conditions hold, f measures position from that axis in the direction of motion.

Replacing unresolved directions introduces relative state errors on the order of this tolerance. Energy is classified as parabolic only within 8 machine epsilons of the local kinetic/potential energy scale. Radial motion is detected within 8 machine epsilons of zero transverse velocity; the plane is chosen deterministically about the outward ray. Near zero energy, relative accuracy of a and state reconstruction from rounded a, e degrades as machine epsilon divided by abs(1-e). Nonradial finite-energy states whose eccentricity rounds to one or the wrong side of one cannot be encoded consistently and are rejected.

Parameters:

  • mu – positive gravitational parameter (L^3/s^2)

  • rVec – nonzero inertial position vector (L)

  • vVec – inertial velocity vector (L/s)

  • canonical – if True, use the C-compatible parabolic encoding; otherwise retain the original Python a = -rPeriap, rApoap = -1 result

Returns:

orbital elements, with distances in L and angles in radians; a, e, rmag, alpha, rPeriap and rApoap retain NumPy float64 scalar arithmetic

Raises:

ValueError – if the state is invalid or its elements are unrepresentable

orbitalMotion.rv2elem_parab(mu: float, rVec: ndarray, vVec: ndarray) → ClassicElements[source]

Convert a Cartesian state using the legacy Python parabolic encoding.

The computation and singular-angle conventions are those of rv2elem(). Planar parabolas return a = rApoap = -rPeriap. Rectilinear states have rPeriap = 0; use elem2rv() to reconstruct radial hyperbolas because elem2rv_parab() retains its negative-a planar encoding. Node, periapsis and nonradial true anomaly retain signed angles in [-pi, pi]; circular orbits place periapsis at the current position. Radial elliptic anomaly retains the supported inward-positive convention.

Parameters:

  • vVec – inertial velocity vector (L/s)

Returns:

orbital elements, with distances in L and angles in radians

orbitalMotion.solarRad(A: float, m: float, sunvec: ndarray) → ndarray[source]

Computes the inertial solar radiation force vectors based on cross-sectional Area and mass of the spacecraft and the position vector of the planet to the sun.

Note

It is assumed that the solar radiation pressure decreases quadratically with distance from sun (in AU)

Solar Radiation Equations obtained from Earth Space and Planets Journal Vol. 51, 1999 pp. 979-986

Parameters:

  • A – Cross-sectional area of the spacecraft that is facing the sun in m^2.

  • m – The mass of the spacecraft in kg.

  • sunvec – Position vector to the Sun in units of AU. Earth has a distance of 1 AU.

Returns:

arvec, The inertial acceleration vector due to the effects of Solar Radiation pressure in km/sec^2. The vector components of the output are the same as the vector components of the sunvec input vector.
